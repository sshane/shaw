Shaw bundled samples: credits and sources
==========================================

Every recording in this folder is in the public domain under Creative Commons CC0 1.0
Universal (https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). The Bass Guitar and the five
guitars come from Karoryfer Samples (Growlybass, Black And Green Guitars, Emilyguitar, and
Shinyguitar; see the end of this file). The vocals come from Freesound (see [Vocals] below).
The String Section, String Stabs, and Brass Stabs come from VSCO 2 Community Edition (see
"String Section, String Stabs, and Brass Stabs" below). Everything else comes from the Versilian Community Sample Library (VCSL) by Versilian Studios
LLC and its contributors.
No rights are reserved; attribution is not required but is given here with thanks.

  VCSL:              https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL
  Versilian Studios: https://versilian-studios.com/

The Pipe Organ and the Knight upright piano were sampled by Simon Dalzell of Ivy Audio
(organ: Winter 2015-16; upright: for the VSCO 2 Pro sample set).

Processing for Shaw: leading silence trimmed, tails trimmed with a short fade, one gain
per instrument (drums and percussion normalized per file), converted to 16-bit Apple
Lossless CAF at the original sample rate. The Kalimba notes were retuned by resampling
(by -36 to +36 cents) to equal temperament, because the source instrument uses its own
traditional tuning. Instrument files are named by the MIDI note of the recorded pitch.

sound's own page on 2026-10-19. Cut into notes, high-passed, retuned by resampling to equal
temperament (by the cents shown), faded, normalized, and converted to 16-bit Apple Lossless CAF.

String Section, String Stabs, and Brass Stabs
=============================================

Source: VSCO 2 Community Edition (VSCO 2: CE), an orchestral sample set by Versilian Studios
and Sam Gossner. The String Section layers the cello, viola, and violin sections playing
sustained notes with vibrato, cellos for the low notes, violas for the middle, and violins on
top. The String Stabs use the same three sections playing spiccato. The Brass Stabs use a tenor
trombone for the low notes and a trumpet from C4 up, both playing staccato.

  Repository:        https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE
  Commit used:       440300901dfe9275fd84e0b7763af1f8443ae62e (branch master)
  Versilian Studios: https://versilian-studios.com/
  License:           CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain dedication), per the repository's LICENSE.
                     Its readme asks for credit to Versilian Studios and Sam Gossner, given here.

Processing for Shaw: one take per note from the loudest dynamic layer, leading silence
trimmed so each note starts at once, sustained notes cut to 6 seconds and stabs to at most
1.5 seconds, tails faded, resampled from 44.1 kHz to 48 kHz, and converted to 16-bit Apple
Lossless CAF. Each note has its own gain so a scale plays evenly: sustained notes match by
integrated loudness, and stabs match by their loudest 100 ms. A limiter takes up to 4 dB off
the sharpest trombone attacks to keep them from clipping. Pitch analysis puts every note
within 15 cents of equal temperament, so none needed retuning. VSCO calls middle C "C3", one
octave lower than MIDI's usual C4, so its C3 files are saved as 060.caf.

Source paths are relative to the root of the VSCO-2-CE repository (branch master).

                     "Copyright 2014 Karoryfer Lecolds."

Shaw uses the sustained notes (no release samples, staccato notes, or pick scrapes), the "f" velocity
layer (the second loudest of four), and round robin 1. The library names its files one octave above
the sounding pitch, so e2_f_rr1.wav sounds E1 (MIDI 28).

long and still ringing at its end, so each file keeps the full 6 s with a fade over the last 10%;
one gain (+0.2 dB) for the whole instrument so the loudest file peaks at -1 dBFS; 16-bit with TPDF
dither; Apple Lossless in CAF; 44.1 kHz mono, as recorded. Shaw's file names give the sounding MIDI
note.

Processing (all three instruments)
----------------------------------
Converted from 24-bit WAV to 16-bit (TPDF dither) Apple Lossless CAF at the original
44.1 kHz mono. Each file starts 2 ms before the pick attack (the source files begin
right at the attack, so the lead-in is digital silence) with a 1 ms fade-in. Tails
are trimmed once they stay below -65 dBFS with a 30 ms fade, or capped at 6 s with a
fade over the last 10% when the note still rings. One gain per instrument, so
relative levels between notes are the library's own: Clean Electric Guitar
+3.98 dB, Acoustic Archtop +2.02 dB, Jazz Guitar +1.43 dB.
No pitch, timbre, or other changes.

readme.txt of karoryfer.emilyguitar
===================================

Epiphone Emily the Strange guitar played and mapped by D. Smolken.

SFZ mappings included:
Basic - all samples, nothing fancy
Clean - as above minus string muting samples on note release
Three voice - three round robins at once
Five voice - five voices created by pitch-shifting four higher samples down to match the note being played
Chords - power, minor and major barre chords with keyswitches
Chords wide - as above but with the strings spread out in stereo

Standard tuning though the low E string was also sampled tuned down to Db. The strings are d'Addario Chromes flatwounds, 11 gauge. All samples recorded direct, using both pickups and all knobs on ten.
Notes have four velocity layers and three round robins, release samples four round robins, noises five round robins.

Royalty-free for all commercial and non-commercial use.
Copyright 2014 Karoryfer Lecolds.


readme.txt of karoryfer.shinyguitar
===================================

Shinyguitar is a sample library for Plogue Sforzando version 1.822 or newer. It contains samples of an archtop guitar played and mapped by D. Smolken.

This instrument is open source, and you can do whatever you want with it; however, the XML bank contains a digital signature, and editing the XML bank will break the ability of Plogue Sforzando to load the instrument with the GUI.

To register, drag the Shinyguitar.bank.xml file onto the Sforzando interface. Once registered, Shinyguitar will be available in the Sforzando loading menu.

MIDI CC control assignments:
7 - volume
10 - pan
100 - blend (0 = all magnetic pickup signal, 127 = all microphone signal)
101 - tone (lowpass filter cutoff)
112 - tame (sample offset)
110 - mute (simultaneous envelope hold, decay and sustain adjustment)

1 - vibrato depth
103 - vibrato speed
104 - vibrato onset delay
105 - vibrato onset fade-in

107 - number of voices (one plus release noises, three without release noises or five without release noises)
106 - release noise volume
108 - stereo width for extra voices

Photos by Arkadiusz Kożuch.
